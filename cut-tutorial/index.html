CUT Tips

If you are playing against unskilled opponents, no complicated strategy is needed. Look for a weakness or wait for mistakes and take advantage.

Sets happen because of overbids or poor card selection during play, and occasionally because of short suits—none of which you can count on or control. In other words, against skilled opponents, sets don't happen because you planned them. Take advantage when they happen, but planning for them is a mistake. What will generally happen instead is not a set but you taking all or most of the bags. Instead, with skilled opponents, assume they have bid correctly and that a set is unlikely since, if they are truly skilled, that will generally be the case.

Between two skilled teams, then, the deciding factor, besides pure luck, is the bag game. Playing to set means ignoring bags. If your opponents do not ignore bags, they have an automatic advantage.

Bottom line: now that you've improved your game enough to be often making semifinals and finals, it's time to adjust your strategy for matches against the best teams. Stop thinking you can win simply by being aggressive. They will spot this, be careful not to overbid, and beat you with a superior bag game.
